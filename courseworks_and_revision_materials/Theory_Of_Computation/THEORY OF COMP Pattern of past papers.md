Question 1)	ALWAYS 50 MARKS in total

Le goat Loc's part


>  a)	ALWAYS 10 MARKS in total

      Usually about propositional logic, and/ or boolean algebra

      i)	ALWAYS 3 MARKS
	
	Explain whether some propositional formula is satisfiable and whether its valid
	Sometimes it only asks for validity (seen in 2022)

      ii)	ALWAYS 3 MARKS
	
	Convert some formula into another
	Usually it asks to express some boolean algebra as a product of sums, simplifying as far as possible
	Sometimes it asks for converting propositional logic into CNF form (seen in 2022)
	

      iii)	USUALLY 4 MARKS, IF iv) EXISTS THEN 3 MARKS (seen in 2022)

	Usually consider some new propositional connective.
	  and show standard connectives can be defined with that connectives (seen in 2025 and 2024)
	  or give standard propositional formula equivalent to formula with the new connective (seen in 2021)

	Or write a recursive function that converts a propositional formula to an equivalent formula
	  using only connectives (seen in 2022), or boolean algebra (seen in 2019)  
	  
	Or explain why a statement causes problems for principle of excluded middle and non-contradiction (seen in 2022)
	
      iv)	ALWAYS 1 MARKS (sometimes this question is omitted)
	
	Explain how to fix issues with principle of excluded middle and non-contradiction (seen in 2022)


>  b)	ALWAYS 10 MARKS in total

      Always given some first order language
      Five questions are usually some english sentence you need to translate to formulae or sentences of Σ in M

      i)	ALWAYS 2 MARKS
      ii)	ALWAYS 2 MARKS
      iii)	ALWAYS 2 MARKS
      iv)	ALWAYS 2 MARKS
      v)	ALWAYS 2 MARKS


>  c)	ALWAYS 10 MARKS in total

      Quite spread out topics on proofs, can be either 1 big question up to 3 sub questions

      May include
	Prove formally some first-order formulae is not valid (seen in 2022, 2022 LSA, 2021)
	Prove formally some first-order structure is valid in all valuations (seen in 2019, 2025)
	Explain and prove equivalence or non-equivalence for two first-order formulae (seen in 2021)
	Consider an english sentence, formalise it into a first-order formula (seen in 2024)
	Define a first-order structure that turns a formula valid, explaining why it is valid in that structure (seen in 2024)
	Explain whether a formula is generally valid (seen in 2024)
	

>  d)	ALWAYS 10 MARKS in total

      Sometimes it's a standalone 10 mark question (seen in 2022 LSA, 2019), more often its two (seen in 2021, 2024, 2025) or three subquestions (seen in 2022)
      IF IT'S A STANDALONE 10 MARK QUESTION, its ALWAYS asking to prove by structural induction on the recursive grammar/ function

      Usually a definition of a recursive functions or grammars (lwk just haskell) like lists (twice), binary trees, fibonacci
      Sometimes gives definition of grammar of propositional logic formulae
      Sometimes asks for the inductive definition of terms of a first-order language (seen in 2022)


      i)	USUALLY 8 MARKS IF THERE ARE TWO SUBQUESTIONS, 2 MARKS IF THERE ARE THREE SUBQUESTIONS
	
	May include
		Give inductive definition for terms of first-order language (seen in 2022)
		State structural induction principle for the recursive grammar/ function/ formulae
		Prove by induction on the recursive grammar/ function

      ii)	USUALLY 2 MARKS IF THERE ARE TWO SUBQUESTIONS, 3 MARKS IF THERE ARE THREE SUBQUESTIONS

	May include
		State structural induction principle for terms of first-order language (seen in 2022)
		Prove by structural induction on the recursive grammar/ function/ formulae
		Explain why proof by structural induction doesn't work well on a grammar (such as lists) (seen in 2021)
		
      iii)	ALWAYS 5 MARKS (usually this question is omitted)
	
	Prove by structural induction on a first-order structure M with domain D and valuation rho for M,
	for all terms t on some valuation update (seen in 2022) 


>  e)	ALWAYS 10 MARKS

      Usually 2 subquestions, but sometimes it could be one big standalone question (seen in 2019)
	
      Always given modal logic frame M = (W, R) and a property and the definition of the property

      IF IT IS A STANDALONE QUESTION, it ALWAYS a prove some modal logic formula is valid in M if it follows the property defined prior

      i)	ALWAYS 5 MARKS

	Prove some modal logic formula is valid in every frame with the property previously defined

      ii)	ALWAYS 5 MARK
	
	Prove some modal logic formula is valid ONLY in some frames with the property previously defined


Question 2)	ALWAYS 50 MARKS in total	

the jappie's part


>  a)	ALWAYS 10 MARKS in total

      Usually a standalone question, but can be 2 subquestions (seen in 2022)

      May include
          Creating a DFA that recognises some regular expression (seen in 2022 LSA) or language (seen in 2022)
          Expressing/ converting a DFA into a regular expression (seen in 2024, 2021)
	  Converting an NFA to an equivalent DFA (seen in 2025, 2019)

      i)	ALWAYS 5 MARKS (usually this question is omitted)
	
	Create a DFA capable of recognising a language (seen in 2022)

      ii)	ALWAYS 5 MARKS (usually this question is omitted)

	Convert a DFA into a regular expression using the GNFA reduction method


>  b) USUALLY 10 MARKS in total, can be only 5 marks (seen in 2021)

      Usually a standalone question, but can be 2 subquestions (seen in 2019)

      May include
          Given a language and its alphabet
	      Either create a PDA that recognises the language (seen in 2022)
	      Or prove the language is or is not regular (seen in 2025, 2024, 2021)
	
          Given a grammar
	      Tell and provide a derivation on whether some strings are generatable with the grammar (seen in 2019)
              Provide the language generated by the grammar in set notation (seen in 2019)

	  Given an NFA
	      convert it into a DFA (seen in 2022 LSA)

      i) ALWAYS 4 MARKS (usually this question is omitted)

          Given a grammar and some strings, provide justification on whether or not they can be generated (seen in 2019)

      ii) ALWAYS 6 MARKS (usually this question is omitted)

	  Give a grammar, describe the language generated by it in set notation of the set of generatable strings (seen in 2019)


>  c) USUALLY 10 MARKS in total, can be only 5 marks (seen in 2021)

      Usually a standalone question, but can be 2 subquestions (seen in 2021)

      May include
          Given a language
	      Either create a CFG capable of generating the language (seen in 2022)
	      Or prove whether it is regular or non-regular (seen in 2022 LSA, 2019)
	      Or create a PDA that recognises the language (may be asked to not use the generic CFG-to-PDA construction) (seen in 2025, 2024)

	  True or false questions with justification (may not require proofs)
	      On context-free language
	          (may be asked about if any long string in a CFL must have an infinitely pumpable substring) (seen in 2021)
	      On turing machines
	          (may be asked if set of decidable languages contain finite-string-length languages as well as infinite-string-length languages) (seen in 2021)

      i)

	  True or false on a property of context-free languages with a justification required (no proof needed) (seen in 2021)

      ii)

	  True or false on a property of Turing decidable languages with a justification required (no proof needed) (seen in 2021)


>  d)	USUALLY 10 MARKS in total, can be 20 marks if previous questions have lower marks (seen in 2021)
       
	Usually a standalone question, but can be 2 subquestions (seen in 2025, 2022 LSA, 2021)

	May include

	        Prove if it is context-free or not (seen in 2022, 2021, 2019)
		Create a context-free grammar that generates it (seen in 2024)

		Tell whether some strings are generatable with it, and give a derivation (seen in 2025, 2022 LSA)

	    Given a grammar, tell whether some strings are generatable, if so give a derivation (seen in 2025, 2022 LSA)

	ii) SOMETIMES 6 MARKS (seen in 2025, 2022 LSA) and SOMETIMES 10 MARKS (seen in 2021)

	    Given a language, prove it is or is not context-free (seen in 2021)
	    Given a grammar, provide the language generated in set notation (seen in 2025, 2022 LSA)
